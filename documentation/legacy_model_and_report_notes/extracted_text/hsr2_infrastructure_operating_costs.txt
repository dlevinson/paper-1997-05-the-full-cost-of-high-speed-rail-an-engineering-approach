
2. Infrastructure and Operating Costs of Existing High Speed Rail System






2.1 Infrastructure Costs

South-East TGV

The southern section of the French South-East TGV high speed line (170 miles) came into service in 1981. At this time the travel time from Paris to Lyon was 2 hours and 40 minutes. The northern section of the new line (71 miles) opened to traffic in 1983 and provided a reduction in travel time of 40 minutes. The estimated cost of the new line was 2,901 Millions of French Francs (1975 prices) while the actual cost was 2,926 millions of French Francs, indicating an overrun of only 1%. The overrun for the rolling stock was 3.8%. The assessment of investments for the infrastructure, the superstructure and the South-East TGV fleet are presented in Table 2.1.

Atlantic TGV

The Atlantic TGV was also commissioned in two stages : the western branch (180 kilometers) between Paris and Le Mans towards Rennes, Nantes and Brittany in September 1989 and the southwestern branch (101 kilometers) between Courtalain junction on the Paris-Le Mans high speed line and Tours towards Bordeaux, Tarbes, Toulouse and Spain in September 1990. The assessment of investments for the infrastructure, the superstructure and the Atlantic TGV fleet are presented in Table 2.1. Construction costs have amounted to ECU 1.45 billion, less than 2% in excess of project estimates. Atlantic TGV rolling stock is highly superior technologically in terms of performance (power rating of 8,800 kW against 6,450 kW for the South-East TGV fleet), capacity (485 seats compared with 368) and comfort. Maximum revenue-service speed for Atlantic TGV trainsets is 300 kph on the new lime, compared with 270 kph for the South-East TGV (1,991 km have been covered at over 400 kph and 696 km at over 450 kph).

2.3 The Structure of High Speed Rail Operating Costs


A study conducted by INRETS and INTRAPLAN provided estimates of the average high speed rail operating cost for Europe. In this study, operating costs were divided into the following breakdown :

	- sales and administration,
	- shunting,
	- train operations,
	- maintenance of way,
	- maintenance of equipment, and
	- energy.

Sales and Administration

Sale and administration costs include labor costs for ticket sales and for providing information at the railroad stations. They also include costs for automated ticketing machine  and travel agency commissions. In the INRETS/INTRAPLAN study, sale and administration costs have not been estimated on the basis of the required number of staff and automated ticketing machine for a given level of expected traffic volume but have been assumed to represent 10% of the passenger revenue.

Sale and administration costs for a high speed rail system in California could be significantly different compared to Europe since the cost of labor as well as the disparity between costs and fares are likely to differ. Thus, it may be more accurate to estimate sale and administration costs with regards to those observed for airlines.


Shunting costs depend on the distance between the depot and the station as well as the average period of time trainsets stay at the depot. Nonetheless, to simplify, shunting costs could be approximate on a per train basis. The study conducted by INRETS and INTRAPLAN has shown that the cost of labor represents 80% of the total shunting cost.

Train Operations

Train operation can be divided into four activities :
	- train servicing,
	- driving,
	-operations and safety on high speed lines, and
	-operations and safety on conventional lines.

Train operation costs consist exclusively in labor costs. Train servicing and driving for the South-East TGV and the Atlantic TGV is two train companions per trainset and one driver per train (which may include one or two trainsets). Operations and safety on either high speed or conventional lines can be estimated on a per train basis.

Maintenance of Way and Equipment

The cost of the maintenance of electric traction installations and catenary depends on the number of trains running on the infrastructure whereas the cost of maintaining the tracks depends on the number of trainsets. Theoretically, the cost of maintenance of equipment is dependent upon the distance run by every trainset as well as the duration of use. In the INRETS / INTRAPLAN study, the impact of the duration of use has been ignored so that maintenance of equipment cost can be estimated on a trainset per kilometer basis.

According to the INRETS / INTRAPLAN study, the proportions of the cost of labor in the maintenance costs are 55% for maintenance of electric traction installations, 45% for maintenance of tracks and 50% for maintenance of equipment.

Energy

Costs can be estimated from the average consumption of energy required per kilometer which characterized the trainsets. The cost of energy is assumed to take into account the cost of transport and the electrical losses between the power generating station and the substations. Operating costs related to energy do not include any labor cost.
